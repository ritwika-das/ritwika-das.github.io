About

I currently tackle health and social care data infrastructure building as a data scientist at the Care Policy & Evaluation Centre (CPEC). For example, I created an open-source package for qualitative and non-technical researchers to access Care Quality Commission API data. The package maintains historical data on a data repository while regularly updating the package’s existing dataset, providing users with recently added changes without making any redundant API calls.

Before CPEC, I worked at the private service provider CLIA Care, where I engaged with the same health and social care clients that my current data work seeks to serve. Here I encountered daily incident reports, behavioural notes, and care plans piling up as unusable data. I grew interested in quantifying and operationalising the complexity embedded in that data. Currently, on a project evaluating and developing LLMs for early safeguarding detection in adult social care records, I leverage my experience in navigating safeguarding hearings at CLIA Care and training in machine learning to fine-tune LLM models.

Beyond my current role, I am interested in learning and sharing new research methodologies and write for GOLTC which is platform for collaborative research at CPEC.

Projects

Replication Data Visualisation
Replication, Das, R., (2025)
I replicated Narasimhan & Weaver's Polity size and local government performance: Evidence from India. My main findings proved this paper is highly replicable across different software implementations and methodological approaches. Despite initial challenges with a clerical error in the code script, bandwidth selection and software-specific functions, the core regression discontinuity estimates remained consistent, demonstrating the robustness of the underlying empircal relationships.

Market Analysis
Social Inequality in Brazillian e-commerce market (2025)
This market analysis examines how socioeconomic and geographic factors influence e-commerce behavior across Brazilian states, focusing on indicators such as income, population, and Human Development Index. It explores how differences in development levels shape consumer preferences, purchasing power, spending patterns, and payment choices, while identifying regional clusters with similar market characteristics. The project also assesses how geography and infrastructure affect delivery performance and trade flows, revealing disparities in logistics efficiency and market accessibility.
